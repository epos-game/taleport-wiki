Metadata is the set of fields a reader sees before they open anything: the title, the cover, the description, the genre and tags, and the classification badges. It decides who finds the story and whether they bother opening it, a reviewer reads it before they read a line of your graph, and the publish dialog holds a chapter back while any required part of it is empty. A branching horror novella set in a Nordic lighthouse carries the title *The Keeper's Log*, a two-sentence pitch that ends on the last radio call, a cover, Horror as its genre, Gothic Novel and Nordic among its tags, and a 16+ recommendation with the Violence content tag. Chapter one then adds a title and a description of its own.

Some of it belongs to the story and covers every chapter in it. The rest belongs to the chapter you are publishing.

## What the story carries

- Title, up to 200 characters.
- Description, up to 1000 characters. This is the pitch.
- Cover image. See [Cover image](/en/media/cover-image/) for what makes a good one, and [Images](/en/media/images/) for formats and rights.
- One [genre](/en/publishing/genres-and-tags/) and at least one [tag](/en/publishing/genres-and-tags/).
- Classification: the [age recommendation](/en/publishing/age-ratings/), the [content tags](/en/publishing/content-labels/), the AI-generated content declaration and the human-authorship confirmation. All four belong to the story, so they describe chapters you have not written yet as well.
- Contributors, each credited with one or more contributions. Six are offered as presets, Author, Translator, Publisher, Editor, Storyteller and Illustrator, and you can type something else. See [Testing and contributors](/en/getting-started/testing-and-contributors/).

Title, description, cover, genre and at least one tag have to be filled in before any chapter of the story can go to review. Your author profile is checked at the same time, and that part is covered in [Publishing requirements](/en/publishing/publishing-requirements/).

## What a chapter carries

- Title, up to 200 characters.
- Description, up to 1000 characters.

The publish dialog wants both before it lets you past the Metadata step. The description is the one authors leave until last. It is what a reader reads when deciding whether to carry on with your story, which makes it worth more than a line restating the chapter title.

## What counts toward the 1000 characters

Both descriptions are written in the rich-text editor, and the counter measures the string that gets stored, markup included. A paragraph break, a bold phrase and a link come out of the same budget as the words. A description that reads as 800 characters of prose can therefore be over the limit: the counter under the editor turns red, the step refuses to save, and the server rejects the same overflow if it ever gets that far.

## Filling it in while you publish

The **Metadata** step puts **Story details** and **This chapter** side by side, next to a live preview card that renders the cover with the title and the genre over it. Until you fill those in it reads "Untitled story" and "No genre yet". Seven fields live in this step: story title, description, cover, genre, tags, chapter title and chapter description. Next stays disabled until all seven have something in them, and the tooltip says why: "Fill in the required chapter details before continuing."

Next is also a save. It uploads the cover you dropped on the preview and writes the story and the chapter immediately, so what you typed survives even if you close the dialog two steps later. The Classification step saves the same way, which matters more there: a content tag you tick and then walk away from is already on the story.

Classification is not part of the Metadata step, and you cannot credit anyone from the publish dialog either. The Contributors step only decides who keeps access to the story once it is published; people are added and credited under Contributors on the story itself.

## Writing a description

Lead with the situation rather than the setting. "The keeper has not answered the radio in nine days, and you are the one they sent up the cliff" does more than three sentences about the island's history. Two or three sentences is the right size, because readers scan. Do not promise what the chapter does not deliver: readers rate each chapter thumbs up or thumbs down, and those votes add up into the story's rating. Leave the feature list out as well, since "branching narrative with meaningful choices" describes every story here.

## Rights on the cover

The cover is held to the same rights requirements as the rest of a story, [CR-II.1](/en/publishing/content-rules/#cr-ii-1). The AI-generated content declaration, [CR-III.2](/en/publishing/content-rules/#cr-iii-2), reads the media inside the story: images on nodes, character avatars and global-event images. The cover is not one of them, so a cover made with AI does not switch the AI Image chip on by itself.
