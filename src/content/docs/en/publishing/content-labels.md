Content labels warn readers about what is in your story. There are six. The app calls them **content tags**. You set them in the **Classification** step of the publish dialog, next to the [age recommendation](/en/publishing/age-ratings/), under *Content tags (optional)*.

- Labels belong to the story, not to one chapter. One scene with blood puts **Violence** and **Blood** on the whole story.
- Labels are optional. You can submit a chapter and get it approved with none.

## The six tags

The app shows only the label name. The descriptions below come from this wiki.

| Tag | What belongs under it |
| --- | --- |
| Violence | Physical conflict, injury, combat, death |
| Blood | Visible or described blood and gore |
| Profanity | Strong or frequent swearing |
| Horror | Fear, dread, disturbing imagery, body horror |
| Sexuality | Sexual themes, nudity, sexual references |
| Sensitive topics | Suicide, self-harm, abuse, addiction, serious mental distress and similar |

## Adding and removing

- **Add** tags at any time.
- **Remove** tags only until your first chapter goes live. After that, tags can only be added.
- A live chapter that is back under review still counts as live.
- When removal is locked, the option is greyed out. The tooltip says *This tag can't be removed once a chapter is live.*

## Saving

The **Classification** step saves when you press **Next**, not when you submit. A tag you tick stays on the story even if you close the dialog. It becomes permanent when any chapter goes live.

## Choosing tags

- A tag says what is in the story. One scene is enough.
- The age recommendation says how old a reader should be. Tags say what the content is.
- Use **Sensitive topics** for suicide, self-harm and abuse, even if the story treats them only briefly. [CR-I.6](/en/publishing/content-rules/#cr-i-6) allows handling these subjects responsibly. The tag warns readers first.
- Do not mix these up with the story tags in the [Metadata](/en/publishing/story-metadata/) step. Those say what the story is about and help readers find it. A story can have both.

## Keeping tags accurate

You choose content tags yourself. Readers see exactly the tags you choose, so go through them carefully before you publish a chapter.

After publication, a reader can report the story as **Misclassified**, one of seven reasons. The report is about the story, not a chapter or one tag. TalePort reviews it. The app never changes your story because of a report. [CR-VI.1](/en/publishing/content-rules/#cr-vi-1) says what TalePort may do.
