Content tags are six warnings you put on a story to say what is inside it. The app calls them **content tags** and they sit next to the [age recommendation](/en/publishing/age-ratings/) in the **Classification** step of the publish dialog, under the heading *Content tags (optional)*. They belong to the story, not to one chapter. One node called *The Cellar*, where a guard bleeds out over four lines of dialogue, therefore puts **Violence** and **Blood** on the whole story, including the three chapters that never leave the market square.

Optional is literal. A chapter can be submitted and approved with none of them set. Set them anyway, for the reasons below.

## The six tags

The app shows the label and nothing else, with no description under it. What belongs under each one is your judgement; these are the readings this wiki uses.

| Tag | Set it when the story contains |
| --- | --- |
| Violence | Physical conflict, injury, combat, death |
| Blood | Visible or described blood and gore |
| Profanity | Strong or frequent swearing |
| Horror | Fear, dread, disturbing imagery, body horror |
| Sexuality | Sexual themes, nudity, sexual references |
| Sensitive topics | Suicide, self-harm, abuse, addiction, serious mental distress and similar |

## They can be added, never removed

Once any chapter of the story is published, including one that is published and back under review, a tag you have already set is permanent. The chip greys out and the tooltip says why: *This tag can't be removed once a chapter is live.* Adding more is allowed at any time.

That makes the two mistakes asymmetrical. A tag you did not need is a small permanent inaccuracy. A tag you left off you can add whenever you notice. Nothing is locked until your first chapter goes live, so that is the moment to get the set right; after it, tag what the story contains today and add to the set when a later chapter earns it.

## Next saves them, Cancel does not undo it

The Classification step writes to the story when you press **Next**, not when you submit. Tick **Horror**, move on to Pricing, then close the dialog and abandon the submission: Horror is already on the story, and it turns permanent the moment any chapter goes live. Read the row back before you leave the step.

## How to use them

- Tag what is present, not what is prominent. One scene counts.
- The tags and the age recommendation do different jobs. A 16+ story still has to say *why* it is 16+.
- **Sensitive topics is the one authors most often miss.** If your story deals with suicide, self-harm or abuse, even briefly and even well, tag it. Handling those subjects responsibly is permitted under [CR-I.6](/en/publishing/content-rules/#cr-i-6). Letting a reader walk into them unwarned is a different thing.
- These are not the story tags from the [Metadata](/en/publishing/story-metadata/) step. Those say what your story is about and help readers find it. Content tags warn them what is inside. A horror story wants both.

## What happens when they are wrong

Nothing checks them. The publish checklist has no row for content tags and the publish job never looks at them. Review will not catch a mismatch either: the classification is shown on the admin story page and in the read-only chapters panel, and both of those need the Admin role, so a reviewer working through the chapter queue has no screen listing what you declared. The set is read after publication, by readers.

After publication a reader can report the story from the EPOS app, and **Misclassified** is one of the seven reasons on offer. The report is filed against the story rather than against a chapter or a single tag. A moderator then closes it as resolved or not relevant and can leave an internal note for other moderators. No part of the app changes your story because of a report; what TalePort may do about one is set out in [CR-VI.1](/en/publishing/content-rules/#cr-vi-1).
