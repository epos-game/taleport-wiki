A rejected chapter comes back with the reviewer's notes. Each note is pinned to a node, often to a component inside it.

- **Reject** stays disabled until the chapter has at least one note that did not come from a tester.
- The rejection email repeats the first few notes and gives the total.
- A never-published chapter comes back as an editable draft.
- A live chapter comes back as *Published · revising*, with its readers and its locked graph. See [Story lifecycle](/en/getting-started/story-lifecycle/#rejection). If the fix needs a structural change, see [Published content restrictions](/en/publishing/published-content-restrictions/).

Below are the usual reasons a chapter comes back.

## Rights to media

You used an image, track or clip that you have no right to publish. See [CR-II.1](/en/publishing/content-rules/#cr-ii-1), [CR-II.2](/en/publishing/content-rules/#cr-ii-2).

## Wrong age recommendation

Your content fits a higher category than the one you chose. See [CR-IV](/en/publishing/content-rules/#cr-iv).

The strongest moment anywhere in the story sets the rating. It belongs to the story, not to one chapter. Once any chapter is live, you can only raise it. See [Age recommendation](/en/publishing/age-ratings/).

## AI-generated text

The story text, dialogue or choice text was produced by generative AI. See [CR-III.1](/en/publishing/content-rules/#cr-iii-1).

The text itself is prohibited, and no declaration changes that. The **Classification** step will not save until you tick the human-authorship checkbox. You may use AI only for spelling, grammar and translation of text you wrote.

## A false AI declaration

**AI-generated content** in the **Classification** step has four options: AI Image, AI Music, AI Speech and AI Video. You can pick only media your story contains. The rest are greyed out. Leaving an option off for AI-generated media is a false declaration. See [CR-III.2](/en/publishing/content-rules/#cr-iii-2). If it looks deliberate, see [CR-III.4](/en/publishing/content-rules/#cr-iii-4).

The declaration also affects the recommended price, so one mistake shows up twice.

## Unfinished content

Your story has something the editor warns about. [Story guidelines](/en/publishing/story-guidelines/) lists every warning. Check for placeholder text (such as *TODO* in a dialogue or filler text) and temporary artwork yourself before you submit. A reviewer looks for both. The [interactive preview](/en/story-editor/node-preview/) helps you find them.

## Missing media files

This one never reaches a reviewer. A file your story uses but no longer has shows up only during processing, after you submit. Processing fails, and the chapter comes back with no verdict. See [Non-blocking items](/en/publishing/publishing-requirements/#non-blocking-items).

Re-upload or remove the files the **Missing media files** dialog lists, then publish again.

## Missing or wrong content tags

A content tag is missing or wrong, most often *Sensitive topics*. See [Content labels](/en/publishing/content-labels/). You add a tag with one click. Once a chapter is live, you cannot remove the tag.

## Prohibited content

Your content contains something listed in [Article I](/en/publishing/content-rules/#cr-i). A difficult subject is allowed as part of the narrative. It is prohibited when the content promotes it, teaches it or exists for gratification. The rule itself is the standard, not this summary.

## After a rejection

Read every note, fix what the notes point to, and replay the branches you changed. Then resolve the high-priority notes ([Priority and approval](/en/publishing/review-feedback/#priority-and-approval)) and submit again. A chapter that fell back to draft offers **Publish**. A live chapter offers **Republish** ([Republishing](/en/getting-started/publishing/#republishing)).

If you think the decision was wrong, write to TalePort support. Do not resubmit the same chapter ([CR-VII.1](/en/publishing/content-rules/#cr-vii-1)). The full procedure is in [Working with review feedback](/en/publishing/review-feedback/).
