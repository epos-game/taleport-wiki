A rejection is a status change plus notes, and nothing else is recorded. The chapter comes back to you with each note pinned to the node it is about, often to one component inside it. Submit chapter three with a music track off a free-samples site and two dialogue lines that still read "TODO", and it returns as a draft carrying three notes: one on the *Harbour at dusk* node, two on the lines beneath it. No written verdict arrives with them, which is why a reviewer cannot send a chapter back without writing at least one note.

Where it lands depends on whether readers already had it.

- **A chapter that was never published** comes back as an editable draft. Everything is open again.
- **A chapter that is live** comes back as *Published · revising*. Readers keep the version they have, you edit component content and resubmit, and the graph stays frozen. If the fix needs a structural change, see [Published content restrictions](/en/publishing/published-content-restrictions/).

TalePort records no cause of rejection anywhere, so the sections below are not a ranking. They are the rules a chapter has to clear, plus the gaps the editor lets through.

## Rights to media

An image, a track or a clip you do not hold the right to publish. See [CR-II.1](/en/publishing/content-rules/#cr-ii-1), [CR-II.2](/en/publishing/content-rules/#cr-ii-2).

*Avoid it:* write down where every asset came from and under what licence as you add it. Reconstructing that for forty images after a rejection is miserable. "Found online" and "royalty-free site, did not read the terms" are the two usual failures.

## Wrong age recommendation

Content that belongs in a category above the one you chose. See [CR-IV](/en/publishing/content-rules/#cr-iv).

*Avoid it:* rate for the most extreme moment anywhere in the story, not for the overall tone. The rating sits on the story rather than on this chapter, and once any chapter is live it can only be raised. A correction here is permanent. See [Age recommendation](/en/publishing/age-ratings/).

## AI-generated text

Story prose, dialogue or choice text produced by generative AI. See [CR-III.1](/en/publishing/content-rules/#cr-iii-1). The text itself is prohibited, so this is not a question of disclosure: no declaration makes AI-written prose publishable, and the Classification step refuses to save until the human-authorship checkbox is ticked.

*Avoid it:* use AI for spelling, grammar and translation only, on text you wrote.

## A false AI declaration

**AI-generated content** in the Classification step is four chips: AI Image, AI Music, AI Speech and AI Video. Only media your story actually contains can be picked; the rest sit disabled. Leaving a chip off for media that was generated is a false declaration. See [CR-III.2](/en/publishing/content-rules/#cr-iii-2), and [CR-III.4](/en/publishing/content-rules/#cr-iii-4) if it looks deliberate.

The declaration also feeds the recommended price, so one inaccuracy shows up twice.

## Unfinished content

Everything the editor warns about and still lets through: a node nothing reaches, a node with no content, a text component with no text, dialogue lines with no character or no text, a switch condition with no requirements, a background or music component where nothing is set and nothing is inherited. Placeholder text and temporary artwork are not detected at all. A reviewer reads them.

*Avoid it:* clear the warnings before submitting, not only the errors. [Story guidelines](/en/publishing/story-guidelines/) sorts one from the other.

## Missing media files

This one never reaches a reviewer. Nothing in the publish dialog looks at storage, so a file your story references but storage no longer holds travels through all seven steps untouched. The background job then fails, the chapter drops back to the status it had, and the Publish button opens **Missing media files** with a row per file labelled *In a node*, *Story cover image*, *Character avatar* or *Global event*. Each row jumps to the file.

*Avoid it:* re-upload or remove what the dialog lists, then publish again. **Publish without these files** strips those references out of the package, so the scene plays with no background and the character appears with no avatar.

## Missing or wrong content tags

Particularly *Sensitive topics*. See [Content labels](/en/publishing/content-labels/). A tag takes one click to add and cannot be taken off once a chapter is live, so decide it properly rather than in reaction to a rejection.

## Prohibited content

Anything in [Article I](/en/publishing/content-rules/#cr-i). Most of those rules share a shape: the difficult subject is permitted as narrative, and prohibited where the content promotes it, instructs in it, or exists for gratification. If you are writing near one of those lines, read the rule itself rather than the summary.

## After a rejection

1. Read every note. They are the decision, and there is nothing else to read.
2. Fix what they point at, then replay the branches you touched.
3. Clear the high-priority notes. Resolving one, lowering its priority and deleting it all unblock approval, and none of the three does the fix. Any unresolved high-priority note blocks it, including one your own tester wrote.
4. Submit again. A chapter that fell to draft offers **Publish**; a live one offers **Republish**, disabled with *No changes to republish yet* until you have actually changed something. A rejection does not release the reviewer's claim, so the chapter goes back to the person who sent it.
5. If you think the decision was wrong, write to TalePort support instead of resubmitting unchanged. See [CR-VII.1](/en/publishing/content-rules/#cr-vii-1).
