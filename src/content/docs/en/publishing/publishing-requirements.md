Before TalePort takes a chapter for review it checks your author profile, the story the chapter belongs to, and the chapter itself. The publish dialog's first step, **Validation**, headed *Ready to publish?*, runs fourteen of those checks and shows the result as pass-or-fail rows, so nothing surfaces for the first time on the submit screen. Open it on "Chapter 2: The Salt Road" with no cover image and no chapter description and the top of the list is two failing rows, each with a **Review metadata** button; fill both in on the next step and they move down into the group below.

Failing rows come first, under **Needs attention**. Passing rows follow under **Ready**. Most failing rows carry an action that takes you to the fix: **Open profile**, **Open billing settings**, **Review metadata**, **Open chapters**. Two carry none, because there is nowhere to send you: *Story graph has playable content* and *Reachable end-of-chapter ending*.

For a chapter in Draft or Testing the dialog opens one screen earlier, on *How do you want to publish?*, where you choose between a private test build and going to review. What a reviewer then judges is a separate matter: that is the [Content Rules](/en/publishing/content-rules/) plus the [Story guidelines](/en/publishing/story-guidelines/).

## Your author profile

Three rows have nothing to do with the story and catch most first-time authors: **Author display name**, **Author bio** and **Author avatar**. All three hold you on the Validation step until they pass. The bio check strips HTML before it decides, so a bio that contains only empty formatting still counts as missing.

**Billing and payout information** is the fourth profile row and the one exception on the list. It fails whenever your details are incomplete, and a free chapter submits anyway. Only a paid chapter is actually stopped by it.

## The story

Set once per story, and it covers every chapter in it.

- **Story title**, **story description** and a **cover image**
- Exactly one [genre](/en/publishing/genres-and-tags/), and at least one [tag](/en/publishing/genres-and-tags/)
- An [age recommendation](/en/publishing/age-ratings/) that covers the most extreme content anywhere in the story
- Human authorship confirmed: the checkbox stating that the text was written by a human author and not generated by AI. See [CR-III.1](/en/publishing/content-rules/#cr-iii-1).

The last two never appear as rows on the checklist. You set them on the **Classification** step, whose Next button stays disabled until you have chosen a rating and ticked the box, and the server checks both again the moment you submit.

That step saves as you leave it. Next writes the age recommendation, the content tags, the AI declaration and the authorship confirmation to the story immediately, and cancelling the dialog afterwards takes none of it back. Once any chapter of the story is live, the rating can be raised but not lowered, and a content tag already on the story can no longer be removed. Both appear as greyed-out chips with a tooltip saying why.

## The chapter

- **Chapter description**, plus a chapter title, which the Metadata step requires before it lets you continue.
- At least one ending reachable from the start and marked **End of chapter**. A chapter can have many [end nodes](/en/story-editor/end-nodes/); this check asks whether a reader can finish the chapter rather than only a branch.
- **No graph errors.** Warnings do not block, errors do, and every error gets its own row. A row offers **Go to node** only when the error belongs to a node, so a missing Start node or an End node nothing can reach shows up as a bare failing line. See [Story guidelines](/en/publishing/story-guidelines/).
- *Story graph has playable content.* The price calculation needs the longest route a reader can walk to come out above zero minutes, and an almost empty chapter fails here.
- *Earlier chapters are ready for review.* Chapters go to review in order, so every chapter ahead of this one has to be live or already in review.

Four of those rows hold you on the first step: the reachable ending, graph errors, playable content and chapter order. The chapter description counts as metadata, so it waves you on, as do the story metadata, a missing age recommendation and incomplete billing. Later steps are where you fix those.

## What does not block a submit

- [Content tags](/en/publishing/content-labels/): optional, and nothing on the checklist looks at them.
- Contributors. That step only decides who keeps access to the story once the chapter is published.
- Billing details on a free chapter.
- Validation warnings: an unreachable node, a node with no content, a backdrop or track with no asset behind it. The editor flags them and lets you submit.

Missing media files are the trap in that list. Nothing in the dialog looks at storage, so the submit goes through and the background build is where it falls over: the chapter comes back to you with *Publishing failed and the chapter was returned to you*, and a dialog then lists each missing file with a jump to where the story uses it. **Publish without these files** reopens the publish dialog with the problem waived, which means walking the seven steps a second time.

## What you declare in the dialog

- **Free or paid**, and a price if paid. See [Free and paid content](/en/monetization/free-and-paid-content/).
- **AI-generated content.** Four chips under that heading: AI Image, AI Music, AI Speech and AI Video. Only kinds your story actually contains can be toggled, the rest are greyed out with "Your story doesn't contain this kind of media.", and image, music and speech start selected. This is the declaration required by [CR-III.2](/en/publishing/content-rules/#cr-iii-2), and it feeds the recommended price: a kind you declare human-made counts for more than the same kind generated with AI.
- Rights to every image, track, recording and clip. Nothing in the publish flow verifies this. The rule is [CR-II.1](/en/publishing/content-rules/#cr-ii-1) and it is settled in review.

The authorship checkbox is the only AI-related control that can stop a submission. The four chips never do.

## Charging for a chapter

Two conditions, both about you rather than about this story:

- You are a **Qualified** author, or an active partner.
- Your billing and payout details are complete.

Neither applies to a free chapter. Qualification arrives on its own once one of your free chapters is approved and that single chapter holds at least twenty minutes of unique content; two ten-minute chapters do not add up to it. You can also write to TalePort and ask. See [Monetization requirements](/en/monetization/requirements/) for how the time is measured.
