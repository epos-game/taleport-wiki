Before TalePort accepts a paid chapter it checks two things about you: your author stage and your payout details. Until both are in place the charge toggle in the publish dialog stays greyed out, and free chapters are all you can submit. So you release chapter 1, *The Mire Crossing*, free, with 24 minutes of total reading time in it and an End of chapter ending. A reviewer approves it, you fill in a bank account and your tax details, and the toggle on chapter 2 comes alive with a recommended price already filled in.

Neither check touches free chapters. Those you can publish from the day you arrive.

## How you become Qualified

**Publish one free chapter with at least 20 minutes of content in it, and get it approved.**

Four details decide whether a chapter counts:

- The 20 minutes is one chapter's own content. Three seven-minute chapters do not add up to it.
- The chapter has to be free, approved and published. One sitting in review has not qualified you yet.
- It is the chapter's total content, not one playthrough. Everything in the graph counts, including branches a given reader never sees and nodes nobody can reach.
- Every approved free chapter is measured, not only your first. Miss the threshold on chapter 1 and chapter 4 can still qualify you.

If your chapter is all text, 20 minutes means 20,000 characters, a little over eleven standard pages.

### What counts as content

TalePort measures each node once, taking the first of these that the node has:

1. **Text**, counted by length. A thousand characters is a minute.
2. **Voice-over**, if there is no text: the running time of the recordings attached to the text and the dialogue lines.
3. **Cutscene video**, if there is neither.

Two consequences follow. Narration adds nothing to a node that already carries text, so a node with 2,000 characters and a two-minute recording counts as two minutes rather than four. Background music, ambient sound and looping background video do not count at all; they play under the scene for as long as the reader lingers, so they have no single running time to measure.

## Payout and billing details

Qualified on its own is not enough. A paid submission is refused while your billing is incomplete, and both halves are needed: a bank account and a tax and billing profile. You can delete either of them later from the same page, and doing so quietly takes paid publishing away again. See [Payout process](/en/monetization/payouts/).

Incomplete billing also shows up earlier than you might expect. The publish checklist always carries a **Billing and payout information** row with an *Open billing settings* button, and it reads as failing even when the chapter you are submitting is free. It will not block that free submission. It only stops a paid one.

## Asking to be qualified

The free chapter is the usual route, not the only one. The Pricing step offers a link to write to support@epos.games and request qualification, and TalePort can enable paid publishing without the standard requirement where it sees reason to. That is at its discretion. Article V of the Terms and Conditions says plainly that there is no entitlement to it.

## The stages behind the scenes

TalePort tracks where you are as an author, and the paid-publishing check reads that value. The stage names themselves live in the admin view of authors, but one of them reaches you anyway: the Pricing step tells you that you will be able to charge "once you're a Qualified author", and a paid submission that gets past the dialog comes back with "You must be a Qualified author before you can publish paid content."

| Stage | How you reach it |
| --- | --- |
| Registered | You have an account. |
| In production | You created a story, or a chapter of yours entered review. |
| Published | One of your chapters is published. |
| Qualified | A qualifying free chapter was published, or TalePort marked you as Qualified. |
| Active partner | Your first paid chapter was published while you were Qualified. |
| Terminated | TalePort ended the partnership. |
| Expired | Nothing in TalePort moves an author here. |

Stages only ever move forward. Both Qualified and Active partner clear the paid-publishing check, so nothing is lost by moving on from one to the other. Terminated sits above Qualified in that order, which means marking a terminated account as Qualified does nothing at all.

## If your chapter should have qualified and did not

Check that it is free, that it is published rather than waiting in review, and that its content really runs to twenty minutes. The chapter list shows a **total reading time** per chapter, and that is the figure being measured. The longest path and the average path are different numbers and are not what counts here.

The check runs in the background after approval and logs its own failures rather than reporting them. If all three things hold and your stage has not moved, nothing in the UI will tell you why. Write to support.
