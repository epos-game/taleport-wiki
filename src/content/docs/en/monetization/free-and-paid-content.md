Every chapter is sold on its own, and you pick free or paid for each one separately. The choice is recorded at the moment you submit the chapter for review, so it is not something you have to settle while you are still writing. Say chapter one of The Lighthouse Keeper runs 40 minutes, costs nothing, and ends with the reader bolting the hatch at the node Storm Watch. Chapter two picks that scene up and costs 69 EPS, which a reader who liked the first one pays once.

A free chapter goes straight into a reader's library when they take it. A paid chapter costs them [EPS](/en/monetization/credits/), the in-app currency, and buying it twice is not possible: a second purchase of a chapter the reader already owns is refused.

## Does a story need a free chapter?

Nothing in the publish dialog requires one. A paid submission checks two things, your author stage and your billing details, and neither of them looks at chapter numbers. A Qualified author can charge for chapter one.

There is one obligation, and it is narrower than most people assume: **the free chapter you qualified with has to stay free**, unless you agree otherwise with TalePort. That comes from Article V of the Terms and Conditions, not from a validation rule you will bump into in the editor.

There is still a practical case for one. If every other chapter is paid, the free one is the only writing a reader can judge before spending anything. Nothing in TalePort measures how well that works, so what goes into it is a writing decision rather than a setting.

## Setting a price

The Pricing step of the publish dialog asks "Do you want to charge for this chapter?". Switch it on and the price field, the slider and the presets open up: TalePort computes a recommended price from the chapter's own content and you move it inside a permitted range. Switch it off and the stored price drops to 0. See [Pricing](/en/monetization/pricing/).

The toggle stays greyed out until you are a Qualified author, and a paid submission is refused while your payout and billing details are incomplete. See [Monetization requirements](/en/monetization/requirements/).

## Changing your mind later

Free or paid is not a decision you make once. A published chapter can be submitted again, and you can flip it either way when you do. It keeps its released package while review runs, so readers are not cut off in the meantime.

Readers who already own a chapter keep it. Turning a free chapter into a paid one takes nothing away from someone who already has it, and charges them nothing.

## Early access

When you submit, you can set an early access date. That date is when backers can start reading, and every other reader gets the chapter 7 days later. The dialog says it plainly: "Backers can read the chapter from this date, while all other readers get access 7 days later."

The date has to be at least 14 days out and the server rejects anything sooner, because review itself can take that long.

Leaving the date empty does not hand the chapter to everybody at once. Approval fills the date in with the moment of approval, so backers get the chapter straight away and all other readers 7 days after that. Nothing skips the window.

Early access comes from the Backer role or from a backer tier above Founder. Founder on its own counts as ordinary public access.

The one thing a re-submission cannot change is this date. The calendar is still there and still takes a date, but on a chapter that is already live the server keeps the original. Free or paid and the price do change.
