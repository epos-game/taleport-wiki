Four screen recordings, in the order you meet the app: the story, the editor, the people and the memory inside a chapter, then the chapter itself. They show you the shape of the work, and the page linked under each one has the exact labels and limits. Watch all four and you can take a ghost story called *The Lighthouse at Vardø* from an empty library to a chapter that opens on a node named Arrival, forks into two ways into the building and closes on an ending marked **End of chapter**, which is the one thing review will not pass a chapter without.

These pages also open inside TalePort, from the editor header's **Help** menu under **Documentation**, or with F1 anywhere in the editor. The panel drops embedded players, so a lesson shows only its **Watch on YouTube** link there.

## Lesson 1: Creating a story

<iframe class="video-lesson" src="https://www.youtube-nocookie.com/embed/DE5AgeGlwso?rel=0"

A story is the shell you publish under one author profile: title, description, cover, genre, tags, and at least one chapter. The lesson goes through the four steps of the **Create New Epos** dialog, where a title and a genre are the only compulsory fields, and stops where TalePort drops you afterwards: in the editor, inside an empty **Chapter 1**.

[Watch on YouTube](https://youtu.be/DE5AgeGlwso) · [Read more: Creating a story](/en/getting-started/creating-a-story/)

## Lesson 2: Editor tour

<iframe class="video-lesson" src="https://www.youtube-nocookie.com/embed/_SXjc4tCP_I?rel=0"
        title="Lesson 2: Editor tour" loading="lazy" allowfullscreen
        allow="accelerometer; clipboard-write; encrypted-media; picture-in-picture"></iframe>

The editor is one chapter's graph on a canvas. Nodes and components are dragged in from the palette on the left, the selected node opens on the right under **Components**, **Outputs**, **Preview** and **Notes**, and the bottom panel holds what the whole story shares: **Characters**, **Variables**, **Stats** and **Global Events**. Watch this before you start moving things around. The editor needs a desktop or a tablet in landscape, and the twelve-step walkthrough built into it can be replayed from the **Help** menu with **Show editor tutorial**.

[Watch on YouTube](https://youtu.be/_SXjc4tCP_I) · [Read more: The story graph](/en/story-editor/story-graph/)

## Lesson 3: Characters, stats, and variables

<iframe class="video-lesson" src="https://www.youtube-nocookie.com/embed/SzYJnSrgB3Q?rel=0"
        title="Lesson 3: Characters, stats, and variables" loading="lazy" allowfullscreen
        allow="accelerometer; clipboard-write; encrypted-media; picture-in-picture"></iframe>

Characters are who speaks. Variables are what the story remembers: an event component changes one, a requirement on a transition reads it, and a character's stats are variables hanging off their sheet. In the lighthouse chapter, `Trust` starts at 0, the keeper's first civil answer raises it by 1, and the door at the top of the stairs only opens at 3.

[Watch on YouTube](https://youtu.be/SzYJnSrgB3Q) · [Read more: Variables](/en/story-editor/variables/)

## Lesson 4: Building the story

<iframe class="video-lesson" src="https://www.youtube-nocookie.com/embed/KZISUtXtd6c?rel=0"
        title="Lesson 4: Building the story" loading="lazy" allowfullscreen
        allow="accelerometer; clipboard-write; encrypted-media; picture-in-picture"></iframe>

A node is one screen for the reader: its components are the text, the dialogue, the image and the sound, and its output decides where the reader goes next. The lesson joins those up into something readable, which comes down to three decisions repeated over and over. A continue where nothing branches, a choice where it does, and an ending that finishes the chapter instead of only the current flow.

[Watch on YouTube](https://youtu.be/KZISUtXtd6c) · [Read more: Transitions and choices](/en/story-editor/transitions-and-choices/)
