Publishing a chapter hands it to a TalePort reviewer, not to readers. The publish dialog collects what is still missing, what the chapter will cost and when it goes live, then queues a background build that repackages your released story. Submit "Chapter 3: The Flooded Mine" at 50 EPS on a Tuesday and it joins the review queue; once a reviewer approves it, readers on the Visionary tier start reading that evening and everyone else seven days later.

## Before the button does anything

The publish dialog opens on a **Validation** step split into **Ready** and **Needs attention**. These are the items that actually stop the submission:

- Your author profile: a display name, a bio and an avatar.
- The story: a title, a description, a cover image, a genre and at least one tag.
- This chapter: a description, a graph with no errors, and at least one ending marked **End of chapter** that a reader can reach.
- A measurable path through the chapter. TalePort takes the longest route a reader can walk; the check fails if that route comes out at zero minutes, or if the chapter's total duration is shorter than it. The checklist calls this one **Story graph has playable content**.
- The chapters before it: every one of them already live or already in review. A chapter you only test-built does not count, and a chapter you withdrew back to Draft re-blocks everything after it.

Two blockers are missing from that list because you set them further along: the age recommendation and the human-authorship confirmation, both on the **Classification** step.

The **End of chapter** requirement catches a mistake that is easy to miss. If every ending a reader can reach only closes off a branch, they can finish a path but never finish the chapter, so the story has no way to carry them into the next one. Open an End node and turn on **End of chapter**. See [End nodes](/en/story-editor/end-nodes/).

**Billing and payout information** sits on the same checklist but only blocks a **paid** chapter. A free one submits without it.

## The dialog

For a chapter in Draft or Testing, the dialog first asks what you are doing: build a private test package for your contributors, or go to review. Testing is the cheaper order of operations; see [Testing and contributors](/en/getting-started/testing-and-contributors/). A chapter that is already live skips the question, because testing only exists before release.

Review runs through seven steps: **Validation**, **Metadata**, **Classification**, **Pricing**, **Early access**, **Contributors**, and a **Review** summary with the submit button. While blocking issues remain you move forward one step at a time. The moment validation reports the chapter ready, every step becomes clickable and you can jump straight to the summary.

## Classification

The **Classification** step sets properties of the **story**, not of this chapter, even though you reach it from a chapter. You answer these once for everything you publish under that story:

- An **age recommendation**: 7+, 12+, 16+ or 18+. See [Age recommendation](/en/publishing/age-ratings/).
- Optional **content tags**: violence, blood, profanity, horror, sexuality, sensitive topics. See [Content labels](/en/publishing/content-labels/).
- A declaration of **AI-generated content** per kind of media: images, music, speech and video. Only the kinds your story actually contains can be marked; the rest are greyed out with "Your story doesn't contain this kind of media." Required under [CR-III.2](/en/publishing/content-rules/#cr-iii-2), and misdeclaring it is itself a violation ([CR-III.4](/en/publishing/content-rules/#cr-iii-4)).
- A confirmation that the text was written by a human and not generated by AI. You cannot submit without it.

Go through the AI toggles even if you generated nothing. Images, music and speech start switched **on** for every kind of media your story contains, so a story whose classification you never opened declares all of it as AI-generated. Each toggle works both ways: switching it off declares human production, which also raises the recommended price.

Two settings harden once any chapter of the story is live. The age recommendation can be raised but not lowered, and an existing content tag cannot be removed. Adding a tag stays open.

## Pricing

A chapter is free or paid; see [Free and paid content](/en/monetization/free-and-paid-content/).

Charging needs two things: the **Qualified** author stage or an active partnership, and complete billing and payout details. Qualification arrives on its own when you publish one approved **free** chapter carrying at least 20 minutes of unique content. Several shorter chapters do not add up to it. You can also ask for it by writing to support@epos.games, which the pricing step links for you. Until then the step explains where you stand and leaves the chapter free.

For a paid chapter TalePort computes a recommended price from the chapter's content and gives you a band around it: half the recommendation at the bottom, never below the 9 EPS platform minimum, and 299 EPS at the top. Presets for **50%**, **Recommended** and **Max** sit above a slider, and you can type an exact figure instead. Prices are in EPS, the in-app currency, at 1 EPS = 1 CZK, roughly €0.04. See [Pricing](/en/monetization/pricing/).

If a reviewer previously adjusted your price, resubmitting clears that adjustment and uses your figure again.

## Early access

There is always a gap between approval and general availability. The **Early access** step decides when the clock starts, not whether it runs.

- Pick a date and readers on the Visionary tier and above can read the chapter from that date. Everyone else gets it seven days later, Founder-tier backers included.
- Pick nothing and the date becomes the moment of approval, with the same seven-day gap after it.
- A date you pick has to be at least 14 days out, because review itself can take that long.

The date is only written while the chapter is not live yet. Resubmitting a published chapter never moves it, so a released chapter keeps the schedule it shipped with.

## Contributors

The **Contributors** step chooses who stays on the story once this version is published. Unselecting someone removes their access to future versions and keeps the feedback they already left. The creator cannot be unselected, and people credited without an Epos account are shown but not selectable.

## What submitting actually does

The chapter enters the review queue and TalePort queues a background build. Closing the dialog is not the end of the work: the button shows **Queued…** and then **Processing…**, and a message tells you when the build finished or failed.

- Publishing rebuilds the whole published story. Every chapter that is already live or holding a review slot is repackaged into this new version alongside the one you submitted, and that includes chapters you only test-built. Drafts you have not published are left alone.
- One publish at a time per story. Start a second while the first is running and TalePort tells you to wait for it to finish.
- A failed build is not a lost chapter. It comes back in the status it had, flagged as having unpublished changes. The build retries up to three times on its own, and after 20 minutes a stuck one is written off.

A build can also fail because your story points at media that is no longer in storage. A dialog lists those files by where they are used, in a node, the story cover image, a character avatar or a global event, and offers **Go to node** or **Publish without these files**. Test builds skip missing media, so this only ever surfaces on a review submission.

While a reviewer holds the chapter the editor opens read-only. You do not have to wait for the verdict: **Withdraw from review** pulls the chapter straight back, to Draft on a first submission or to Published if it was already live. You will find it in the chapter list, in the menu on the publish button, and on the **In Review** tab of your **Dashboard**.

The decision also reaches you by e-mail. An approval carries both availability dates; a rejection carries the summary and the reviewer's comments. See [Review process](/en/publishing/review-process/) for what the reviewer checks and how long it takes.

## Republishing

The button reads **Republish** whenever the chapter has been submitted before and has not fallen back to Draft. Withdraw a first submission, or have it rejected before it was ever live, and the chapter returns to Draft with the button reading **Publish** again. Either way it only fires when there is something new to send; greyed out with nothing to send means your last change was already submitted.

## Before you submit

Run through [Preparing content for review](/en/best-practices/preparing-for-review/). Most rejections are for things the author could have caught in ten minutes.
